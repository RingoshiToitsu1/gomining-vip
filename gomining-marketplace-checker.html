How the payback is worked out

A Greedy Machine's hashrate grows by a small percentage every week, for free. The checker turns that into one question: how long until the free TH covers what you overpaid?

  • Premium: the asking price minus what the same hashrate costs at GoMining's going $/TH for its efficiency. That's the part of the price only growth pays back.
  • Free growth: compounds weekly on the machine's current TH, and each new TH is valued at the efficiency you run it at, at full price: if GoMining is running a sale, most of the growth arrives after it ends.
  • Growing it: growth is a percentage, so a bigger machine grows more TH a week. Topping it up at the going rate adds no premium, it just makes the growth big enough to repay the premium sooner. The checker assumes no efficiency upgrade unless you pick one, and shows any upgrade as its own cost.

For every payback target from 1 to 12 months, the checker solves for the size the machine needs to be, what the top-up costs, and the total you'd put in. Manual top-ups stop at 5,000 TH, so a target that needs more than that is marked as out of reach.

size × ((1 + weekly growth)weeks − 1) × $/TH = premium

Why efficiency decides most marketplace deals

The electricity fee is charged per watt, so a 20 W/TH miner pays two thirds more electricity fee than a 12 W/TH one for the same hashrate. That gap is permanent unless you pay to upgrade it away, which is why the checker prices both the 15 W and the 12 W upgrade.

Upgrades are bought one W/TH step at a time, and the steps get more expensive the closer you get to 12:

Stepping down$/TH per W/TH step
50 → 35 W/TH$0.10
35 → 28 W/TH$0.50
28 → 20 W/TH$1.00
20 → 15 W/TH$1.10
15 → 12 W/TH$2.67

So the first watts off a very inefficient miner are cheap: a 35 W/TH miner gets to 28 for $3.50 per TH, which cuts its electricity fee by a fifth. That's why an old 25–35 W/TH miner can still be a bargain at the right price, even though the full trip to 12 W/TH costs about $25 per TH.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a Greedy Machine take to pay back its premium?

It depends on the premium, the machine's size and its weekly growth. The premium is the asking price minus what its hashrate costs at the going rate. Growth is a percentage of the machine's TH, so a bigger machine repays the same premium faster. Paste the listing into this checker and it shows how many TH the machine needs for a 1 to 12 month payback, and what topping it up costs.

Should I upgrade the efficiency of a miner I buy on the marketplace?

Upgrades are bought one W/TH step at a time, and the price per TH depends on where the miner starts: $0.10 a step from 50 down to 35 W/TH, $0.50 down to 28, $1.00 down to 20, $1.10 down to 15, and $2.67 for each of the last three steps to 12. Taking a 20 W/TH miner to 12 costs $13.51 per TH. It pays off when the electricity fee it saves each year is a better return on the upgrade cost than the miner earns on its own price. The checker works this out for the listing you paste.

Why are older GoMining miners cheaper on the marketplace?

Many secondhand miners run at 20 W/TH or more, while new miners are 15 W/TH (12 W/TH is only reachable by upgrading). Every extra watt per TH adds to the daily electricity fee, so an older miner earns less from the same hashrate. A low asking price can still be a bad deal once that fee, or the cost of upgrading it away, is counted.

Does this checker see my GoMining account?

No. It only reads the public listing for the miner number in the link you paste: its hashrate, efficiency and asking price. You never log in, and nothing about your account is requested or stored.
